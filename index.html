Concept

"Winning has been a mystery since day one. We are always in this crime scene hoping and searching for salvation — we will show all the star gates, those good frictions from disagreement and open personalities that lead the way to our breakthrough."

This series is about what pulls us back onto the right path. It shapes how we focus, reminds us of the good we can do, and points us toward the future through nature — God's gift, something we stay connected to so we can reach the blessing we're after and make a dream real. I call this journey from unlucky to lucky (Wezeh).

I've stayed on this path because it's given me freedom, acceptance, guidance — things you lose when you turn away from your own path for someone else's. My work is also about our successes as people with culture, and the dangers we've come through. Things that unite us, protect us. I like sharing that with people.

It's also about what this journey looks like for people around me — giving credit to whoever helped, saying self motivations, thinking ahead about problems before they hit. If our descendants get the same path and the same gift, I believe the foundation leading to the future is very strong. Something that welcomes whatever comes next, even after we're not around to explain it ourselves. Honestly, it's also a way of keeping people out of chaos, away from the kind of influence that leads nowhere good.

The more connected we stay to whatever's protected us across the centuries, the closer our future gets — that's how I see it. My paintings talks about what's increased our chances of winning, from birth until now. Ideas and statements that keep me and the people I love going in this line of work.

My work also turns toward the people who've shaped it — friends, communities, the ones increasing our chances of winning without even knowing it. Building something better around us is what makes development and creativity possible in the first place. And the universe has its own way of reforming things, moving from imperfect toward something closer to whole.

Philosophy

A lot of my focus goes into searching for symbols, titles, behaviours, forms — anything that carries answers that have increased my chances of winning. It matters because it answers prayer, it takes the stress off, and when an answer actually satisfies me, that becomes my breakeven point. It's also how I fight the wicked, whether that's in my own head or coming from outside. A way of learning to battle my own indiscipline, and I like putting that into the work — what it actually looks like to come out on top.

I spend a lot of my life talking about the things we should be protecting and passing down — our traditions, our resources, education, health, community life. These are our weapons, really. Things that unite us and protect us. When a painting reminds people that these are our weapons, we tend to hold onto them instead of letting them slip. We build more of them, because they've carried us this far and we're not about to accept defeat now.

Underneath all of that, I'm just trying to build a mindset that carries some positive influence in the community — and I learn as much from other people as they learn from me, mostly through the paintings themselves. A few traits I keep coming back to:

  • WezehCreate memories that mark the path from unlucky to lucky.
  • DisciplineAcquire disciplined movement from people, and put it into practice.
  • ConnectionBuild relationships with people and ideas that help our growth.
  • AwarenessLearn to recognise opportunities and avoid traps.
  • GrowthLet the findings show real progress in winning.

Really, I'm just trying to put a language to our struggles and our hopes. How does someone actually increase their chances of a meaningful, fortunate life without losing themselves along the way? Even before success shows up, my paintings are there to remind us who and what already got us closer to winning.

The set square holds the whole philosophy in one instrument. Six straight edges, a triangle inside a triangle, angles of 30, 60, 90 degrees. It looks fragile but it's capable of a lot — you just have to handle it well. I think we're the same way. We have to manage our own minds with that kind of care, redirecting our thinking toward the future we actually want. Across the canvas, pointing in every direction, that triangle turns into a map of what's possible for anyone willing to put the work in.

Colour works the same way. Five hues, one family — blue against red, warm against cool — so different palettes still read as one voice. That's basically the name too: RU from Ruler, FI from the five colours, CENT from century. Ruficent. God bless the century that welcomed us.

Inspiration

What's a certificate even worth, next to someone who has none but actually knows how things work and puts it into practice, after all the time and resources and sacrifices it took to get there? My parents always believed I'd grow into that kind of responsibility. They spent what they had putting me on a good path, hoping I'd become resourceful enough to solve problems before they even showed up. Everything I've learned — from teachers, lecturers, doctors, pastors, chiefs, the quietly smart people who show up before you even know to ask — comes back to one thing. Everyone dreams of a good leader, but a good leader is something you train yourself to become. Setting an example when things get hard. Leaving something behind worth remembering.

I keep coming back to the masters. Picasso especially — the courage to break a human figure down to its simplest geometric shapes and show it exactly how he wanted, the same instinct behind those African-mask-inflected faces of his. Vermeer taught me something quieter. Patience with light. He worked in an age when materials were scarce and still left behind around thirty paintings that hold their glow to this day. Limitation wasn't defeat for him, just a different discipline. Learning from both of them is what pushed me toward my own mark — figures cut down to their simplest geometry, built off the same right-angle triangle that runs through the whole Ruficent language, light moving between positive and negative space, colour doing as much psychological work as the subject itself.

I want to keep building resources, push my trade forward, explore more, defend my name as an artist — leave a better footprint for my community and the world. The people who've backed me from day one taught me that good influence actually matters in building a better future. That's the influence I want to keep passing on.

Technical
Quality

Technically it comes down to two things. Repetition — colour, shape, and line showing up again and again — and rhythm, the movement your eye follows because of that repetition. Every Ruficent piece is built off one motif, the right-angle triangle from the set square, with five chosen colours forming one family. That's where the rhythm, the identity, the visual power all comes from.

Repetition

Recurrence of colour, shape and line. The triangle returns again and again, building visual rhythm across the entire canvas.

Rhythm

Visual movement of elements in a repeated order — the eye travels continuously through fields of interlocking geometric form.

Eventry

The right-angle triangle is really the only tool that runs through the whole process. Five colours arranged into one family — blue, red, green, black — so the blend reads as unified at a glance, more than it would if I mixed families. Where families differ is where objects separate from each other, or where light falls differently across a figure. Even small details like the eyes, nose, lips get pushed to stand out and carry weight in the piece.

Colour Families

Five colours form one family. Differences in family schemes separate objects and build how light plays on every figure.

Identity

Rooted in Ghanaian visual culture — the Gye Nyame symbol, the drape of cloth — rendered inside the Ruficent language.

Subject
Matter

At the centre of it all is the hero of change. Resilience, self-motivation, ideas meant to inspire whoever's on this same artistic journey right now.

Content

I'm always trying to develop new techniques — bring more playfulness into the art world, more push into shaping where we're headed. Good intentions attract good solutions, I think. And underneath it all is identity. Not forgetting who we are as Africans, as art lovers, the role we've played in history, and what we're handing down to whoever comes next, in painting and everywhere else.

Interpretation

For me interpretation is about duality. Leaning on good fortune and real resources to push back against whatever pulls people toward misfortune. Thinking outside the box instead of accepting the limit in front of you. It's about giving the next generation more room to play, write, educate themselves — a life spent showing what it actually means to grow smarter, and to build ideas useful enough to help us stand in painting history, and past it. That's what I've spent my life trying to highlight.

Subjective

My focus stays on figurative portraiture, human and animal, alongside still life and landscape.

Relevance

As citizens of this planet, we all carry our own identities and reasons for wanting to build a better future, for ourselves and whoever comes after. My focus is on holding onto the good moments, the good people who helped along the way, and the prayers and intentions worth sharing.

Medicica Bag
A3 (Quarter Imperial) · 12×16 in  ·  Acrylic on Canvas  ·  2023

Born from a level 300 assignment where the lecturer granted freedom of colour and pattern. A crack in heaven — using the right-angle triangle from the set square with deliberate colour selection became the breakthrough. Medicica saved me from continuous trial and error, wasting time and energy searching for local and global recognition. My lecturer loved it. We moved on.

03
Akameho
70 × 40 in  ·  Acrylic & Oil on Canvas

Doctors have tried. Engineers have tried. Billionaires have tried. If it sounds easy, you're doing it wrong — the discouragement was louder than the pressure itself, loved ones calling the brush a punishment for some unnamed karma. I channelled that noise onto the canvas instead of letting it win. Our ancestors faced this same disgrace and stayed anyway; be bold enough to turn it into an answer that educates the ones who doubted you.

Silence is not agreement. We have to react to any oppressive condition we fall within. Proud Ghanaian identity: the Gye Nyame symbol, cloth draped on the shoulder, the Ruficent black background. No One Will Fight Our Battle
8 × 6 ft  ·  Acrylic & Oil on Canvas  ·  2026

A shift from one pattern to another. Before this, survival rested on parents and guardians — now that the degree is earned, it's time to stand firm and fight for our own health, recognition, and freedom.
