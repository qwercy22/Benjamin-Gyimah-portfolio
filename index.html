The Technique

What is
Ruficent?

RUFICENT

A geometric abstract technique built entirely from the right-angle triangle ruler — the set square — as the sole motif. Five carefully selected colours form one unified family, creating rhythm, identity, and visual power across every canvas.

RURuler
FIFive Colours
CENTCentury

God bless the century that welcomed us. Each colour family of five is chosen so they are complementary to another family — blue to red, warm to cool — echoing the idea of different peoples coming together as one unified whole.

Repetition

Recurrence of colour, shape and line. The triangle returns again and again, building visual rhythm across the entire canvas.

Rhythm

Visual movement of elements in a repeated order — the eye travels continuously through fields of interlocking geometric form.

Colour Families

Five colours form one family. Differences in family schemes separate objects and build how light plays on every figure.

Identity

Rooted in Ghanaian visual culture — the Gye Nyame symbol, the drape of cloth — rendered inside the Ruficent language.

01
Medicica Bag
A3 (Quarter Imperial) · 12×16 in  ·  Acrylic on Canvas  ·  2023

Born from a level 300 assignment where the lecturer granted freedom of colour and pattern. A crack in heaven — using the right-angle triangle from the set square with deliberate colour selection became the breakthrough. Medicica saved me from continuous trial and error, wasting time and energy searching for local and global recognition. My lecturer loved it. We moved on.

Akameho
70 × 40 in  ·  Acrylic & Oil on Canvas

Doctors have tried. Engineers have tried. Billionaires have tried. If it sounds easy, you're doing it wrong — the discouragement was louder than the pressure itself, loved ones calling the brush a punishment for some unnamed karma. I channelled that noise onto the canvas instead of letting it win. Our ancestors faced this same disgrace and stayed anyway; be bold enough to turn it into an answer that educates the ones who doubted you.

No One Will Fight Our Battle

Before this, survival rested on parents and guardians — now that the degree is earned, it's time to stand firm and fight for our own health, recognition, and freedom. Artist Statement

Philo­sophy

Theme of the Year

Behind the River — a new open stage in the painting world, working towards local and global recognition by producing art that reflects those who reshape and strengthen the artistic journey.

"Winning has been a mystery since day one. We are always in this crime scene hoping and searching for salvation — we will show all the star gates, those good frictions from disagreement and open personalities that lead the way to our breakthrough."

My art is a manifestation on the way to success — one that benefits contemporary young artists in the way someone like me survived and celebrates our breakthrough from local to global recognition. With a contemporary mindset rooted in the dedication of our inner abilities, I explore the pursuit of honour and achievement.

Each work becomes a reflection of resilience, the power of growth, and the legacy of human potential. Refreshing the mind back on the masters — Leonardo da Vinci perfecting form with dark tones; Vermeer's command of light; Picasso breaking forms into simple geometrics — all their effort helped the art world. I believe there is always the need to solve problems from recurring: new language in building identification, battle strategy, ways of identifying signs. This is what I call pushing the boundary of fear away.

My works turn to show those wonderful people, knowledgeable friends, unique cultural societies that are increasing our chances of winning. Creating a good and better place around us is the condition for development and creativity. The universe has laws of reformation — from imperfect to more perfect formation.

This series explores what motivates us to return to the right path — shaping our focus, reminding us of our positive influence, and building toward our future through the gift and principles of appreciating nature, God's gift from the universe, which we follow and remain connected to in order to achieve blessing and make a dream come true. I call the journey from unlucky to lucky Wezeh. It shapes our focus, reminds us of our positive influence, and lays a foundation that our descendants can follow when we are no longer here to explain it ourselves.

My focus is searching for the symbols, titles, behaviours, and forms that carry the answers that have increased my chances of winning. This matters to me because it answers prayer, it relieves stress, and when an answer satisfies me and supports my journey, it becomes my breakeven point. It creates justice against the wicked — whether that wickedness sits inside my own psychology or comes from social influence — and it's a way of learning new things to battle indiscipline, ideas I love sharing with others through the work: what it looks like to become victorious.

I spend my life preaching the qualities we cherish most — the things we have to stand strong to protect, advance, and nurture in the next generation. These are our weapons of survival: our traditions, our natural resources, education, health, entertainment, social life, and more. Things that unite us, protect us, and carry a positive impact I love sharing with others through the work. When a painting reminds my people that these are our weapons, we hold them close instead of letting them slip away — protecting them, building more of them, because they have increased our chances of winning from untold centuries to today. We can't accept defeat, so we turn the very resources that have carried us this far into something visible.

Our underlying practice is building a mindset that carries a positive influence within our community and among ourselves. I learn as much from others as they learn from me, mostly through the paintings — traits like these:

  • WezehCreate memories that mark the path from unlucky to lucky.
  • DisciplineAcquire disciplined movement from people, and put it into practice.
  • ConnectionBuild relationships with people and ideas that help our growth.
  • AwarenessLearn to recognise opportunities and avoid traps.
  • GrowthLet the findings show real progress in winning.

Observing our struggles and our hopes, and giving them a language — how does a person increase their chances of living a meaningful, fortunate life without losing themselves? Moving forward, even before success arrives, my paintings remind us to honour the individuals and the ideas that have already increased our chances of winning.

The set square carries the whole philosophy in a single instrument: six straight edges, a perfect triangle outside and a smaller one within, angles of 30°, 60°, and 90°. As fragile as it looks, it holds real capability — and it must be handled well to produce a good outcome. I believe we are the same. To be useful, we have to manage our own knowledge, minds, and psychology with the same care, using it to remind ourselves and redirect our own thinking toward building a successful future. Displayed across the canvas, pointing in every direction, the triangle becomes a map of the possibilities open to anyone willing to increase their chances of winning.

Colour works the same way. Five hues are chosen to form a single family — blue paired against red, warm against cool — so that different palettes read as distinct voices coming together as one. It's the same idea behind the name: RU from Ruler, FI from the five-colour family, CENT from century. Ruficent — God bless the century that welcomed us.

Behind every canvas is a debt to the people who made the path possible — teachers, lecturers, doctors, pastors, chiefs, and the quietly intelligent people who show up for you before you know to ask. Everyone dreams of a good leader, but a good leader is one you train yourself to become: setting an example when things get difficult, leaving something worth remembering behind. That's the inheritance I want to build — for my community, and for the next generation of artists coming up behind me.
